Delivery & data notes

A clear boundary around the work.

The public FarmBot example shows the experience. A customer pilot needs its own agreed sources, coverage, reviewer and operating terms.

What is available today

The FarmBot Genesis v1.8 explorer and evaluation repository are public. The example uses public-source designs and documented references. It is independent and has no FarmBot affiliation or endorsement.

The repository provides the published demonstration package under its stated licenses. EXO’s original material has an evaluation license; third-party material retains its own terms. Evaluation access does not grant unrestricted commercial deployment rights. See sources and licenses.

Selectable CAD bodies are not necessarily individually sold replacements. A product reference alone does not confirm applicability, stock or an approved order. Source gaps and revision differences remain relevant.

Start without sending confidential files

Email Blake Grove directly to discuss your machine. The optional pilot brief asks only for a company, equipment model, current parts process and available source formats. All fields are optional. It prepares text in your browser; it does not submit a request or upload files. Downloading or copying the brief is your choice.

The page does not save those entries to EXO storage. Normal web-host request logging is separate from the brief. If you use your mail or messaging application to share it, that service handles the information you send.

Agree a suitable transfer route and permitted use before sharing proprietary CAD or other confidential material. This public page is not a confidential intake system.

Approve the actual customer release

A proposed pilot should name one model revision, the service coverage, the source material permitted for use, and a customer reviewer who can confirm identity, applicability and disclosure.

The customer-facing package should contain only the agreed service representation and fields. Geometry delivered to a browser can be obtained by the visitor; hiding, translucency and a closed panel do not protect it. Original engineering files are not automatically part of the delivery.

Review should apply to the exact package intended for publication. Changed identities, fit rules or disclosed content need affected approvals revisited. These are requirements to establish for a customer engagement, not a claim that this independent example has received OEM acceptance.

Agree responsibility before launch

Work directly with Blake Grove on the proposed scope. Before a paid pilot begins, the written offer should identify:

  • The model/revision, service records and assemblies included, plus unresolved or excluded areas.
  • Setup fee, ongoing fee if any, additional-work rates, review rounds and schedule.
  • The named reviewer, release authority and existing parts destination.
  • Response expectations within the published support hours and the correction/revision allowance.
  • Hosting responsibility, checking cadence, release recovery and who can request a correction or withdrawal.
  • The delivery/export contents, permitted reuse, contract term and arrangements when service ends.

Round-the-clock support, guaranteed response times, automatic monitoring and a continuity provider are not established by this demonstration. Any such commitment must be implemented and agreed separately.

Make handover concrete

The public example is an inspectable evaluation package. It demonstrates one delivery format; it does not establish customer ownership, universal portability or an exit entitlement for a future contract.

A customer agreement should name the data and service assets supplied on exit, their formats and licenses, any hosting dependencies, and the responsibilities for operating them. Request a rebuild and a controlled update/recovery exercise for the agreed delivery before relying on it in service.
